Action-Oriented Projects
A simple way to turn shared purpose into organised, cumulative action
An Action-Oriented Project (AoP) is a public, ongoing project organised around a mission.

It is not primarily an event, a website, a discussion group, or a project-management system.

The work comes first. The gathering accelerates the work.

The basic idea
Most events work like this:

Event → people attend → people talk → event ends

An AoP works differently:

Mission → people → activities → projects → action → evidence → replication

People can begin contributing before any gathering, use a gathering to accelerate decisions and collaboration, and continue the work afterwards.

The project therefore doesn't end when the meeting ends.

What can people actually do?
An AoP can invite people to:

understand the mission
share their knowledge or experience
research a question
identify a problem or opportunity
propose an idea
connect relevant people
build something
test something
document evidence and learning
continue or replicate useful work
These are generic activities, not a rigid programme. Each AoP can adapt them to its mission.

What is produced?
The important outputs are not just presentations or minutes.

An AoP accumulates artifacts:

People — who is contributing and what they can do
Projects — defined pieces of work serving the mission
Actions — concrete commitments and things actually done
Evidence — research, experiments, results, failures and learning
Replication — what others can reuse, adapt or start elsewhere
The project becomes a living record of work, rather than a report about an event.

What about principles?
An AoP can develop principles from the body of work surrounding its mission.

For example, the Humanity AoP began with a large body of existing writing. Recurring themes were identified and developed into fifteen principles.

That is an example, not a prescription.

Any organised body of work could potentially do this — a book, an author's writings, research, an archive, a community's knowledge, an existing movement, or years of accumulated conversations.

The process is:

Organise → publish → discuss → discover themes → challenge them → turn understanding into action → learn → accumulate

The principles themselves can therefore evolve as understanding develops.

Why publish everything cumulatively?
Because important conversations are too easily lost.

An AoP aims to make its evolving work available, connected and cumulative.

A new participant should be able to discover:

What is this about?
What has already been learned?
Who is involved?
What is happening now?
What can I contribute?
What happened when someone tried this before?

The history remains part of the project.

Is it Google Drive? Wikipedia? Jira? GitHub?

Google Drive stores
Wikipedia accumulates knowledge
Slack facilitates conversation
Jira / Trello / Asana coordinate work
GitHub builds collaboratively
A CRM organises relationships
An AoP can use ideas from all of these.

But it starts somewhere else:

There is something worth doing.

The tools and artifacts exist to help people understand that purpose, contribute to it, act on it, learn from it and help others continue it.

Don't organise an event about the work.
